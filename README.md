# 13-week cash forecast template

A free Excel template for the week-by-week cash forecast CFOs use, with a plain-English guide for owners of small and mid-size businesses. Made by [Nori](https://noricap.com), the AI CFO.

**[Download the template (Excel)](nori-13-week-cash-forecast.xlsx)**

## Why 13 weeks?

Thirteen weeks is one quarter. It is far enough ahead to see a tight week coming while there is still time to act (chase a slow payer, move a purchase, line up credit), and close enough that the numbers are mostly known: the invoices you've sent, the bills you've received, payroll and rent.

Profit is not cash. A profitable month can still leave you short if customers pay in 60 days and suppliers want paying in 30. The forecast shows *when* money actually moves.

## How to fill it in

1. **Settings (yellow cells):** your company name, the Monday the forecast starts, today's cash in the bank, and the minimum cash you never want to drop below.
2. **Cash coming in:** what customers will actually *pay* each week, not what you invoice. Start with invoices already sent and when each customer usually pays, then add new sales at your usual payment delay.
3. **Cash going out:** payroll, rent, suppliers, and the unpaid bills already in your books on their due dates (past-due bills in weeks 1 and 2).
4. **What your profit and loss misses:** loan principal, owner withdrawals, estimated taxes and equipment purchases. These are the usual reasons a forecast is too rosy.
5. **Read it:** any week that closes below your minimum turns red. The bottom of the sheet shows your lowest week and how many weeks dip below the line.
6. **Roll it forward weekly:** replace the week just finished with what actually happened and add a new week 13. Comparing forecast to actual is how the forecast gets better.

Blue numbers are inputs you type. Black numbers are formulas: leave them alone.

## Common mistakes

- Counting sales when they're invoiced instead of when they're paid.
- Forgetting quarterly estimated taxes (Apr 15, Jun 15, Sep 15, Jan 15 in the US) and annual bills like insurance.
- Leaving out loan principal, because only the interest shows on the profit and loss.
- Building it once and never updating it.
